## Westchase Homes and the Kitchens Inside Them

Most of Westchase took shape through the 1990s and 2000s, which means the typical kitchen here was finished with oak or maple builder cabinetry, raised-panel doors, and warm wood tones that were standard at the time. Those cabinets were usually built well, with solid boxes and decent hardware, but the honey-oak look and the closed-off layouts can make an otherwise lovely home feel dated. The good news is that solid boxes are exactly what make a kitchen a strong candidate for painting or refacing rather than full replacement.

Because Westchase sits within an active community with an HOA and architectural review board, many homeowners are already thinking carefully about how updates fit the neighborhood. Interior cabinet work does not require board approval, but the same eye for tasteful, lasting finishes carries over. We see a lot of demand for clean white and soft neutral cabinet colors that feel current without chasing a trend that will age quickly.

### Common Cabinet Work Near West Park Village and Countryway

The single most popular project we do in Westchase is [kitchen cabinet painting](/kitchen-cabinet-painting-tampa). Spraying oak or maple cabinets in a durable enamel transforms a 2000s kitchen for a fraction of the cost of replacement, and the factory-smooth finish reads as new cabinetry rather than a paint job. When the door style itself is the problem, [cabinet refacing](/kitchen-cabinet-refacing-tampa) is the next step up: we keep your boxes and layout, replace the doors and drawer fronts, and apply matching veneer so the whole kitchen looks brand new.

For homeowners ready to do more, [custom kitchen cabinets](/custom-kitchen-cabinets-tampa) and a full layout change let us open a closed builder kitchen to the living space, add an island, and build storage around how the household actually cooks. Homes off Linebaugh Avenue and throughout the village often have the footprint for a more open plan once the right cabinetry is designed for it.
